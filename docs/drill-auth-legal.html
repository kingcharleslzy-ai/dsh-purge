4. Applicable laws, articles, and paragraphs

Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, Article 285, paragraph 1: intrusion, in violation of state regulations, into computer information systems concerning state affairs, national defense, or advanced science and technology is prohibited.

Article 285, paragraph 2: intrusion into other computer information systems, obtaining data stored, processed, or transmitted by them through intrusion or other technical means, and unlawfully controlling such systems are prohibited.

Article 285, paragraph 3: providing programs or tools specifically intended for intrusion into or unlawful control of computer information systems is prohibited, as is knowingly supplying such programs or tools to others carrying out those acts.

Article 286, paragraph 1: deleting, modifying, adding to, or interfering with computer information system functions so that the system cannot operate normally is prohibited.

Article 286, paragraph 2: deleting, modifying, or adding to data and applications stored, processed, or transmitted by a system is prohibited.

Article 286, paragraph 3: intentionally creating or distributing computer viruses or other destructive programs that affect normal system operation is prohibited.

Article 287: using computers to commit financial fraud, theft, embezzlement, misappropriation of public funds, theft of state secrets, or other crimes is prohibited.

Article 287-1, paragraph 1, item (1): establishing websites or communication groups for fraud, teaching criminal methods, producing or selling prohibited or controlled items, or other unlawful activities is prohibited.

Article 287-1, paragraph 1, item (2): publishing information about producing or selling drugs, firearms, obscene materials, other prohibited or controlled items, or other unlawful activities is prohibited.

Article 287-1, paragraph 1, item (3): publishing information to facilitate fraud or other crimes is prohibited.

Article 287-2: knowingly providing technical support, advertising, payment settlement, or other assistance to someone using information networks to commit crimes is prohibited.

Article 253-1, paragraphs 1 and 3: selling or providing citizens' personal information to others, stealing it, or otherwise obtaining it unlawfully is prohibited.

Cybersecurity Law of the People's Republic of China, Article 13, paragraph 2: networks must not be used to endanger cybersecurity, national security, honor, or interests; incite subversion of state power, overthrow of the socialist system, separatism, or disruption of national unity; promote terrorism, extremism, ethnic hatred, or discrimination; distribute violent or obscene sexual information; fabricate or spread false information that disrupts economic or social order; or infringe others' reputation, privacy, intellectual property, or other lawful rights.

Article 14: using networks for activities that harm minors' physical or mental health is prohibited.

Article 29: unlawfully intruding into others' networks, interfering with normal network functions, or stealing network data is prohibited. Providing programs or tools specifically intended for network intrusion, interference with network functions or protections, or data theft is prohibited. Knowingly providing technical support, advertising, payment settlement, or other assistance for activities that endanger cybersecurity is prohibited.

Article 33: critical information infrastructure in public communications and information services, energy, transportation, water, finance, public services, and electronic government receives special protection. Intrusion, interference, and destruction without lawful authorization are prohibited.

Article 46: stealing or otherwise unlawfully obtaining personal information, or unlawfully selling or providing it to others, is prohibited.

Article 48: establishing websites or communication groups for fraud, teaching criminal methods, producing or selling prohibited or controlled items, or other unlawful activities is prohibited, as is publishing related information through networks.

Article 50: electronic messages and application software must not contain malicious programs or information whose publication or transmission is prohibited by laws or administrative regulations.

Data Security Law of the People's Republic of China, Article 8: data processing must not endanger national security or public interests or harm the lawful rights of individuals and organizations.

Article 32, paragraph 1: data must be collected lawfully and properly, without theft or other unlawful means.

Article 32, paragraph 2: where laws or administrative regulations specify the purpose and scope of data collection or use, collection and use must remain within them.

Article 51: stealing or otherwise unlawfully obtaining data, or thereby harming the lawful rights of individuals or organizations, is punishable under applicable laws and administrative regulations.

Personal Information Protection Law of the People's Republic of China, Article 5: personal information must be processed lawfully, properly, necessarily, and in good faith, without misleading, fraudulent, or coercive methods.

Article 6, paragraphs 1 and 2: processing must have a clear, reasonable purpose directly related to the processing, using methods with the least impact on individual rights. Collection must be limited to the minimum scope required for that purpose and must not be excessive.

Article 10: unlawfully collecting, using, processing, transmitting, trading, providing, or disclosing others' personal information is prohibited, as is processing personal information in ways that endanger national security or public interests.

Article 13: personal information must not be processed without consent unless another specified basis applies, such as necessity for concluding or performing a contract, statutory duties, emergency response, public-interest journalism or public supervision, processing information lawfully made public by the individual, or another circumstance provided by laws or administrative regulations.

Articles 28 and 29: biometric, medical, financial-account, and location information, and personal information of children under fourteen, are sensitive personal information. Processing requires a specific purpose, sufficient necessity, strict protective measures, and separate consent.

Other effective laws, administrative regulations, regulatory requirements, and the terms of service and abuse policies of the models used must also be followed.
